# Council addendum 1: CDMs and constrained decoding (from the owner, 2026-10-28)

The owner asks the council to research how "CDMs" add to and fit into the debate, starting from https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742. Verify everything below yourself; it is a starting point from the synthesizer, not settled fact.

## Two different things share the name

1. The paper. arXiv 2609.23742, Akash Raosaheb Chavan, submitted 2026-09-20: "Constrained Decoding Eliminates Structural Failures in Small LLMs but Reveals a Scale-Dependent Semantic Gap" ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23742)). It never uses the term CDM. It studies grammar-constrained decoding (CD) of ordinary generative LLMs: five models from 0.6B to 4B (Qwen3-0.6B, Llama-3.2-1B and 3B, Phi-4-mini, Qwen3-4B), 14 hand-designed structured-output tasks, three conditions (native, Outlines, XGrammar), 210 evaluations, greedy decoding. Reported: schema validity rises from 78.6 to 92.9 percent native to 100 percent under CD. Content accuracy rises but not to 1.0 everywhere (Qwen3-0.6B best 0.943). Type-coercion failures are rescued. Instruction-semantic failures are not: on a two-call function task Qwen3-0.6B scores 0.200 native and 0.200 under CD. A "hollow rescue" case scores 1.000 under XGrammar while the email body reads "1. 2. 3. 4. 5.". XGrammar costs 4 to 8 ms to compile a schema and 1.6 to 3.7 percent throughput; Outlines 2.0 to 4.9 s compile and 9 to 13 percent throughput. No significance tests, no calibration or probability analysis, no agents or routing. Its own line: "Schema conformance is necessary but not sufficient for semantic correctness."
2. The product. ScaleDown launched "Constrained Decoding Models (CDMs)" on 2026-10-13: Classify (Classify-1), Extract, Summarize and Compress ([ScaleDown docs](https://docs.scaledown.ai/introduction), [LangChain post](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-long-running-agents-with-scaledown-and-deep-agents), [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/scaledown)). Classify returns a probability per caller-defined label plus a reason. Extract returns spans with confidence and character offsets into the source. Summarize and Compress rewrite or cut context. Vendor claims: 1M-token input, images and PDFs for some models, $0.05 per million input tokens with free output ($0.04 on OpenRouter at launch), "200x faster" and "467x cheaper" than frontier models on these tasks, and "77.2% on DecisionBench vs Jev's 72%". Secondary reports say Classify-1 runs on OpenRouter's Decisions API and is request-compatible with Jev; verify. Open weights, licence, retention and regions are not stated in what I read. One skeptical take: [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/blog/constrained-decoding-models-not-a-new-architecture).

## Why it matters to this debate (hypotheses for you to test, not conclusions)

- The brief listed "an LLM constrained to typed output" as a fallback. The paper is evidence about that fallback: open tools (XGrammar, Outlines) can make a small self-hosted model always schema-valid, which fits the open-source rule for the verification stack. But schema validity is not correctness, and plain CD gives no calibrated probability unless you read token log-probabilities, so thresholds are weak.
- The same lesson applies to Jev and every typed decision model: "never makes a type error" is a structural guarantee, not a semantic one. The driver still needs deterministic checks on meaning.
- ScaleDown adds capabilities System One models lack: query-aware compression and span extraction with offsets, with a 1M-token input. That could feed the superloop's state builder so a Jev request stays under its 32k state-plus-question limit. But Summarize and Compress write text, which puts a generating model, and an injection risk, inside the decision path. Extract spans can be checked against the source by offset; summaries cannot.
- A second typed-decision vendor (Classify-1, and others named in the literature such as an OpenAI typed-decisions endpoint, Laya, Kev, Tev1-4B, Nimble-9B) makes cross-vendor decision voting possible for consequential gates, in the spirit of the independence rule. All accuracy and speed claims here are vendor claims.
- The LangChain post shows a drift detector: classify history after each model call and end the run when the top label is not "progressing" with score above 0.8. That is a superloop no-progress stop.
- Taxonomy for the debate: (a) grammar-constrained decoding of a generative LLM, (b) non-autoregressive typed decision models (System One), (c) ScaleDown's CDM product line, which mixes pointing models and writing models. Keep them apart.
